The Blue Lagoon's QR code and time-slot rules, explained

Since 2025, setting foot on Comino at the Blue Lagoon means registering in advance and showing a QR code at a booked time slot. It's free and fairly simple once you know the steps — but there's a lot of out-of-date and conflicting advice online. Here's how the system actually works in 2026, what each time slot covers, and the one situation where you don't need a pass at all.

Updated June 2026 · ~6 min read

The short version: if you plan to land on Comino at the Blue Lagoon, register beforehand on the official government portal, pick one of three time slots, and bring the QR code you're emailed. There's no ticket to buy — registration is free. If instead you spend the day on a boat at anchor and swim straight from the deck without going ashore, the time-slot system isn't the thing that shapes your day. The rest of this guide is the detail behind those two paths.

Why the rules exist

For years the Blue Lagoon had no limit on numbers, and at the height of summer it showed — the small bay and its strip of shore were packed wall-to-wall by midday. To protect the site and ease the crush, the Maltese authorities introduced an Access Management System in 2025. It caps how many people can be at the lagoon and spreads arrivals across the day using booked time slots. The system is run through an official government portal, and registration is free of charge.

How the QR code and time-slot system works

If you're landing ashore, the process is straightforward:

  1. Register in advance online. Use the official Blue Lagoon access portal. You provide basic details and an email address — there's no charge to register if you're simply setting foot on shore.
  2. Choose a time slot. You select one of the day's slots for your visit (see the table below). Numbers per slot are limited, so popular summer dates can fill up — booking ahead rather than on the day is the safer move.
  3. Get your QR code. Registration produces a unique QR code, sent to you by email. Save it to your phone before you travel — mobile signal on the boat or the bay can be patchy.
  4. Show the code on arrival. Your QR code is checked when you land, and you're given a wristband for your slot. Keep it on for the day.

The three Blue Lagoon time slots

  • Morning — roughly 08:00 to 13:00
  • Afternoon — roughly 13:30 to 17:30
  • Sunset — roughly 18:00 to 22:00
  • Each slot admits a capped number of visitors ashore; registration is free

Reports from the ground suggest enforcement has been inconsistent at times — QR codes not always scanned, time limits not always policed. That may change from season to season, so the safe assumption for planning is that you'll need a valid registration for your slot. Rules and exact slot times can be adjusted year to year, so confirm the current details on the official portal close to your travel date rather than relying on any third-hand summary, including this one.

When you don't need a pass at all

Here's the part most guides bury: the registration applies to people landing on Comino at the Blue Lagoon. If you arrive by private boat, drop anchor in the bay or a nearby cove, and swim straight off the back of the vessel without stepping ashore, you're not landing — so the shore time-slot system isn't what governs your visit. Very brief stops ashore have generally been treated leniently, but that's at the discretion of the officers on duty, so the clean approach is simply to enjoy the water from your own deck.

Landing ashore vs. a day on your own boat

  • Register, book a slot, show a QR code — versus simply anchoring and swimming from the deck
  • Tied to your slot's hours ashore — versus a full day on the water that flexes with the crowds
  • Sharing the bay's shore at the busiest hours — versus moving on to a secluded cove when it fills up
  • Northern ferry timetables — versus a southern cast-off from Ta' Xbiex Creek near Sliema

None of this means the registration system is a problem to be avoided — it's a sensible response to overcrowding, and for most visitors landing ashore it's a quick, free formality. But if you'd rather not plan your day around a booked time slot at all, a private charter is the route that sidesteps it entirely.

Blue Lagoon registration — quick answers

Do I need a QR code to visit the Blue Lagoon in 2026?

If you plan to land on Comino at the Blue Lagoon, yes — since 2025 you register in advance on the official government portal, choose a time slot, and show a QR code on arrival to receive a wristband. Registration is free. If you stay on a private boat at anchor and swim from the deck without going ashore, the shore time-slot system isn't what governs your visit.

How much does Blue Lagoon registration cost?

Registering to set foot ashore at the Blue Lagoon is free of charge — there's no ticket to buy. The Access Management System is about controlling visitor numbers, not selling entry. You'll still pay separately for whatever boat, ferry or tour you use to get there.

What are the Blue Lagoon time slots?

There are three slots across the day: morning (roughly 08:00–13:00), afternoon (roughly 13:30–17:30) and sunset (roughly 18:00–22:00). Each admits a capped number of visitors ashore. Exact times can be adjusted year to year, so confirm the current slots on the official portal close to your travel date.

How far in advance should I register for the Blue Lagoon?

Register before you travel rather than on the day — popular summer slots can fill up, and registering ahead means your QR code is already saved to your phone when you arrive. Mobile signal around Comino can be patchy, so download or screenshot the code before you set off.
